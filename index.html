KEYNOTE SPEECH
Keynote Speech (I)
Utility-Pattern Analysis and Mining

Prof. Jerry C. Lin, Silesian University of Technology, Poland

Abstract

With the rapid growth of data collected daily from individuals, businesses, and various applications, numerous algorithms have been developed to identify interesting and useful patterns that satisfy user-specified requirements. The primary goal of data analysis and data mining is to discover novel and potentially valuable patterns that can be effectively applied in real-world scenarios. For example, analyzing customer transaction data in retail environments can reveal meaningful insights into purchasing behavior, which can subsequently support informed decision-making.

In recent years, there has been a growing demand for utility-oriented pattern mining and analytics, as these approaches are capable of uncovering more useful and informative patterns than traditional binary-based pattern mining methods, such as association rule mining and frequent itemset mining. Utility-oriented techniques have been successfully applied across a wide range of domains, including cross-marketing, e-commerce, finance, and medical and biomedical applications.

In this talk, I will first highlight the advantages of utility-oriented pattern mining and analytics in comparison with traditional approaches. I will then provide a comprehensive overview of the state of the art in utility-oriented pattern mining techniques, organized into three main perspectives: the data level, the constraint level, and the application level. Various techniques and modeling strategies addressing different aspects of utility-oriented pattern mining will be presented and reviewed. Finally, I will discuss how evolutionary models can be integrated into utility-mining tasks.

Biography

Professor Junwei Lin is a distinguished scholar at the Silesian University of Technology in Poland. His research expertise spans data mining and analysis, soft computing, deep learning/machine learning, optimization algorithms, Internet of Things applications, as well as privacy preservation and security technologies. He is the head of the well-known SPMF project, which offers over 200 data mining algorithms and has been widely cited across numerous application fields. Professor Lin has been honored as a "Chinese Highly Cited Scholar" by Elsevier/Scopus in 2018, 2019, and 2020, and was recognized as a "Top 2% Scientist Worldwide" by Stanford University from 2019 to 2024. He is also a Fellow of the Institution of Engineering and Technology (FIET), an ACM Distinguished Scientist, and a Senior Member of IEEE.

Keynote Speech (II)
Meta-heuristic Optimization Algorithms: Improved Strategies and Engineering Application

Prof. Zhenyu Meng, Vice Dean of the School of Computer Science and Mathematics, Fujian University of Technology, China

Abstract

NP-hard problems are not unsolvable; metaheuristic algorithms provide a feasible path to tackling such "impossible" challenges. These algorithms are not some distant "black magic"—they are robust tools for finding high-quality, usable approximate optimal solutions within a reasonable timeframe. Professor Meng Zhenyu from Fujian University of Technology delivered a report titled "Metaheuristic Optimization Algorithms: Enhancement Strategies and Engineering Applications." With a focus on "addressing cutting-edge domains, concentrating on breakthrough innovation, and solidifying practical applications" as the key themes, he shared the latest research findings from his team, unveiling the mysteries behind "impossibility."

Keynote Speech (III)
Evolutionary Ontology Matching

Res. Xingsi Xue, Fujian University of Technology, China

Abstract

Ontology matching plays a pivotal role in enabling semantic interoperability across heterogeneous knowledge systems. Traditional methods, though effective in specific domains, often suffer from limited adaptability, interpretability, or dependence on extensive labeled data. This keynote presents recent advances in evolutionary ontology matching, emphasizing how Genetic Programming (GP) can be leveraged to automate the construction and optimization of similarity features (SFs), classification rules, and aggregation strategies. The first part introduces a Multi-Layer Genetic Programming (MLGP) framework that integrates SF construction, entity pair classification, and alignment aggregation within a unified evolutionary model. By representing individuals as hierarchical trees and employing adaptive fitness functions, MLGP achieves superior scalability and matching accuracy across diverse ontology alignment benchmarks. The second part explores a Collaborative Ontology Matching framework that combines Partial Standard Alignment (PSA) and Active Meta-Learning (AML) to reduce expert supervision and enhance robustness. This framework dynamically updates partial alignments and leverages weighted random forest learning to refine evolutionary search in noisy or incomplete scenarios. Together, these evolutionary paradigms demonstrate a new generation of ontology matching systems that are self-adaptive, interpretable, and capable of continuously learning from evolving semantic environments, thereby advancing the development of intelligent knowledge integration in the Semantic Web.

Biography

Xingsi Xue is a Professor and Researcher at Fujian University of Technology. He received his Ph.D. in Computer Application Technology from Xidian University in 2014, conducted postdoctoral research at Victoria University of Wellington, New Zealand, from 2022 to 2023, and served as a Visiting Scholar at the University of Birmingham, United Kingdom, from 2016 to 2017. His recent research focuses on intelligent computation, knowledge engineering, and complex entity matching methods. He has led or participated in over 40 research projects, including the National Natural Science Foundation of China, the Natural Science Foundation of the Fujian Provincial Department of Science and Technology, and research programs of the Fujian Provincial Department of Education.

He has published more than 300 academic papers in leading international journals and conferences, including over 180 SCI-indexed papers, and has authored two English monographs. He holds over ten granted invention patents. His achievements have been recognized with several honors, including the ACM China Rising Star Award and the Fujian Provincial Excellent Academic Paper Award in Natural Science. He has also been selected as a New Century Excellent Talent of Fujian Higher Education Institutions and an Outstanding Young Scientific Research Talent of Fujian Universities.

He is currently an IEEE Senior Member and a China Computer Federation (CCF) Distinguished Member, serving as Secretary General of the CCF Fuzhou Branch and Vice Chair of the CCF YOCSEF Fuzhou. From 2021 to 2025, he has been consecutively listed among the World's Top 2% Scientists (Annual Impact) released by Stanford University, and since 2023, he has also been continuously included in its Career-Long Impact list. From 2022 to 2025, he has been recognized among the global top 0.05% scholars by ScholarGPS, consistently ranking first worldwide in the field of Ontology research.

Keynote Speech (IV)

Prof. Baojun Qiao, Henan University, China

Abstract

Aiming at addressing issues such as inconsistent system specifications for multi-source heterogeneous remote sensing image data, complex and slow production processes of massive remote sensing products, low data interpretation accuracy in complex scenarios, and insecure remote sensing data sharing and algorithm calling, this study has developed autonomous controllable key technologies for intelligent processing of aerospace multi-source remote sensing data. A "five-layer and fifteen-level" remote sensing data organization system was designed, which supports standardized slicing of multi-source heterogeneous data. An intelligent scheduling and acceleration method for remote sensing products was established, which supports parallel task scheduling in distributed cluster systems. By integrating a process customization method, it achieves efficient full-process production of products on the domestic Sugon platform. A set of deep learning algorithms for intelligent remote sensing image processing was proposed. Targeting different objects in various scenarios, it realizes multi-level interpretation tasks such as classification, detection, recognition, and semantic understanding. A a full-process trusted technology system for remote sensing big data was constructed, which supports the traceability of data sources and the verification of algorithm calling processes, thereby realizing secure sharing of remote sensing data and trusted calling of algorithms. A supporting service platform for intelligent processing of aerospace multi-source remote sensing data with the characteristics of "unification, high speed, intelligence, and trustworthiness" was developed. Based on this platform, services are provided for industries such as environmental protection, energy, agriculture, and water conservancy, achieving favorable economic and social benefits.

Biography

Baojun Qiao is currently a Professor at the School of Computer and Information Engineering, Henan University; Director of Henan Key Laboratory of Big Data Analysis and Processing; and Doctoral Supervisor. He also holds the titles of Distinguished Teacher of Henan Province, Academic Leader of the Department of Education of Henan Province, and Scientific and Technological Innovation Talent in Colleges and Universities of Henan Province. His main research direction is artificial intelligence. Keynote Speech (V)

Prof. Lu Yin, the University of Surrey, UK

Abstract

Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) in artificial intelligence have achieved unprecedented breakthroughs in many fields in recent years, driven by the continuous expansion and improvement of model size and capabilities. However, this strategy of driving performance growth through scale expansion has also brought about increasingly massive computational and storage demands, making the training and inference processes of AI models increasingly resource-intensive. This not only significantly increases economic costs and environmental burden, but also presents challenges in practical deployment, such as energy consumption, hardware costs, and service latency. To address these issues, model sparsity has emerged as a promising solution. By selectively sparsifying the parameters of AI models, it is possible to significantly reduce storage and computational requirements while largely maintaining the model's performance. More importantly, the benefits of model sparsity are not limited to improved resource utilization and efficiency. In the current trend of increasing emphasis on fairness, reliability, and sustainability in AI systems, sparsity methods also help mitigate potential biases in models and lay the foundation for building more socially responsible AI systems in the future. This report systematically explores the multifaceted value of model sparsity in deep learning, from its theoretical foundations to practical applications. Through the discussion of these dimensions, this report aims to provide researchers and practitioners with a more comprehensive understanding of the potential impact of sparsification strategies and offer valuable insights for their widespread application and in-depth development in the future artificial intelligence ecosystem.

Biography

Lu Yin is a tenured Assistant Professor and PhD supervisor at the University of Surrey, UK. He has also served as a long-term visiting researcher at Eindhoven University of Technology and the University of Texas at Austin. He completed both his bachelor's and master's degrees at Harbin Institute of Technology, and later earned his PhD from Eindhoven University of Technology in the Netherlands.

He has published more than 40 research papers in top international AI conferences, including ICML, NeurIPS, ICLR, AAAI, and UAI. He has received the Best Paper Award at the LoG conference and a Best Paper Nomination at ICVS. Dr. Yin maintains extensive collaborations with leading universities worldwide, such as the University of Texas at Austin, the University of Oxford, University College London, and the Max Planck Institute in Germany. In addition, he works closely with major tech companies including Google, Meta, and Intel. His research on AI model acceleration algorithms has already been applied in industry, including by companies like Cerebras and NeuralMagic.

Keynote Speech (VI)

Postdoctoral researcher. Wenzhao Zheng, the Artificial Intelligence Lab of the University of California, Berkeley, USA

Abstract

Autonomous driving systems rely on a complex pipeline that integrates perception, prediction, planning, and control, all of which require substantial computational resources to operate reliably in real time. However, the growing scale of multimodal sensor data and deep neural networks has created significant challenges for on-board computing, including latency, energy consumption, and hardware constraints. This report explores key strategies for achieving efficient computing in autonomous driving, focusing on model compression, hardware–algorithm co-design, edge–cloud collaborative computing, and efficient data processing frameworks. We analyze how lightweight perception models, quantization techniques, and sparse neural architectures can dramatically reduce computational load without compromising safety. Furthermore, we discuss the importance of real-time scheduling, memory-efficient execution, and adaptive resource management within autonomous driving platforms. By integrating algorithmic optimization with emerging AI accelerators and distributed computing paradigms, this report outlines a comprehensive pathway toward scalable, energy-efficient, and safety-critical autonomous driving systems. The findings provide both theoretical insights and practical guidance for advancing next-generation intelligent mobility.

Biography

Wenzhao Zheng is currently a postdoctoral fellow in the Department of EECS at University of California, Berkeley, affiliated with Berkeley Artificial Intelligence Research Lab (BAIR) and Berkeley Deep Drive (BDD). Prior to that, he received the B.S. degree and the Ph.D. degree from the Department of Physics and Department of Automation, Tsinghua University, China, in 2018 and 2023, respectively. He was awarded the Excellent Doctoral Dissertation of Chinese Association for Artificial Intelligence in 2024. His current research interests include computer vision, world models, and embodied agents. He has authored more than 40 papers in TPAMI, CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, NeurIPS, ICML, and ICLR. He serves as a regular reviewer member for a number of journals and conferences, e.g., TPAMI, TIP, TMM, CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, NeurIPS, ICML, and ICLR.

Keynote Speech (VII)
Meta-heuristic Optimization Algorithms with Surrogate Assisted Models

Prof. Jeng-Shyang Pan, Nanjing University of Information Science and Technology, China

Abstract

Meta-heuristic Optimization Algorithms have been shown to be successful for many engineering and commercial applications. However, the expensive computation time of the Meta-heuristic Optimization Algorithms cause the limitations for the real time applications of the many areas. There are several research issues to improve the efficiency of the Meta-heuristic Optimization Algorithms including the hardware implementation and the application of the surrogate assisted models. This talk will focus on the introduction of Meta-heuristic Optimization Algorithms for continuous, discrete, binary and categorical data optimization problems. The surrogate assisted optimization algorithms such as the surrogate assisted models selection, the combination of the Meta-heuristic Optimization Algorithm with the surrogate assisted models will be introduced. The three major branches of the surrogate assisted optimization algorithms, for example the sampling scheme, the simplified fitness function and the dimension decomposition will also be discussed.

He has edited or authored more than 100 books. He is the IET Fellow, UK and has been the Vice Chair of IEEE Tainan Section. He was Awarded Gold Prize in the International Micro Mechanisms Contest held in Tokyo, Japan in 2010. He was also awarded Gold Medal in the Pittsburgh Invention & New Product Exposition (INPEX) in 2010, Gold Medal in the International Exhibition of Geneva Inventions in 2011 and Gold Medal of the IENA, International "Ideas – Inventions – New products", Nuremberg, Germany.
